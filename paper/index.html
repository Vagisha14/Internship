Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

A machine learning approach for prioritizing content pages that may deserve refresh or further human review.

Project type: Content Opportunity Scoring

1. Research Question

Can search-performance, engagement, freshness, and content-related signals be used to prioritize content refresh opportunities, and does a Random Forest model provide useful improvement over a Week-4 baseline?

The objective is to create a decision-support ranking that helps content teams identify pages that deserve manual review.

2. Data

The analysis uses the anonymized content refresh dataset. The dataset contains content, search, traffic, engagement, freshness, and trend-related features.

Important signals include:

  • Search volume and competition
  • Impressions and clicks
  • CTR and average position
  • Sessions and engagement rate
  • Content age and days since last update
  • Recent versus previous performance
  • AI traffic percentage
  • Performance trend percentage

3. Methodology

A Random Forest regression model was selected because the dataset contains multiple numerical content-performance signals that can interact in non-linear ways.

The Week-4 baseline provides a reference ranking. The Random Forest model was then evaluated using held-out data. Identifier fields, precomputed tier fields, and other potentially leakage-prone fields were excluded from the model feature set.

The workflow included preprocessing, feature selection, model training, baseline comparison, honest-split validation, and a leakage audit.

The Random Forest produced lower prediction error than the Week-4 baseline under the reported evaluation setup. This indicates better predictive accuracy on the evaluation data, but it does not prove that refreshing content will improve future performance.

5. Validation and Leakage Audit

An honest holdout evaluation was performed to check how the model behaves when evaluated on observations separated from the training data.

The validation workflow also checked for identifiers, precomputed recommendation fields, tier fields, and other features that could introduce leakage.

The model outputs should therefore be treated as prioritization signals rather than guaranteed predictions.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The system produces a ranked queue of content opportunities. Each recommendation includes a score, a recommended action, and a reason code.

The Top-20 recommendations generated by the Week-4 baseline are used as the initial decision-support queue.

These rankings are intended to help content teams focus their attention on pages that may deserve further investigation.

7. Intended Use and Human Review

The scoring system is intended to support content teams in prioritizing pages for manual review.

Human review is required before acting on any recommendation. A high score does not guarantee that a content refresh will improve performance.

No-Go List

  • Do not automatically publish or modify content based only on the score.
  • Do not treat a high score as proof of future performance improvement.
  • Do not use unreliable or clearly incorrect data.
  • Do not use the model as the sole basis for deleting content.
  • Do not claim causation without controlled testing.

8. Monitoring and Retraining

The system should be monitored as new data becomes available. Monitoring should include data quality, feature drift, ranking stability, model performance, and comparison with the baseline.

Retraining should be considered when model performance declines, feature distributions change substantially, ranking quality becomes unstable, or the model no longer provides useful value compared with the baseline.

9. Limitations

  • The analysis depends on the quality and coverage of the available data.
  • Historical patterns may not represent future search behavior.
  • The model does not establish causal relationships.
  • Model scores do not guarantee successful content refreshes.
  • Human review remains necessary before taking action.

10. Conclusion

This project developed a content opportunity scoring workflow combining a Week-4 baseline with a Random Forest model. The Random Forest achieved lower MAE and RMSE than the baseline under the reported evaluation setup.

The resulting system is best viewed as a decision-support tool that helps prioritize content for human review. Further testing on new observations would be required to determine whether the approach generalizes and whether recommended content refreshes produce measurable business improvements.
